About

Driven by data,
guided by curiosity.

I am a graduating senior at UCLA majoring in Microbiology, Immunology, and Molecular Genetics (MIMG) with a minor in Mathematics, and incoming PhD student in Biomedical Sciences at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai this fall.

My research sits at the intersection of immunology, genomics, and bioinformatics — with a focus on multiomics integrative approaches, single-cell sequencing, and precision medicine. I am drawn to quantitative, data-driven questions: how do immune cells communicate in the tumor microenvironment? What can multimodal data reveal about disease risk and survival?

Beyond research, I lead CURE (Collaboration in Undergraduate Research Enrichment) at UCLA, mentoring 60+ life science undergraduates entering research. I give back to a global high school research program as an alumni and a TA for three years. I am also passionate about sustainability and literature.

Creative Work & Literature

A Literary Side Quest

Alongside science, I find meaning in words — as a reader and as a writer. I am especially drawn to poetry, short stories, and historical fiction: literature that makes the invisible visible.

Bananas, Invisibility, and the Violence of Omission

Academic Essay · Cross-media Analysis · 2023

In this cross-media review of Paul Hlava Ceballos' poetry collection Banana [] and a painting from the virtual exhibition "Banana Craze," I analyze the labor abuse and gendered discrimination endured by Latinx banana workers and African slaves in Latin American banana plantations. Starting from a Chiquita sticker on a dining hall banana, the essay traces how colonialism, sexualization, and racial erasure are embedded in the global banana trade and how artists and poets work to make this invisible history seen.

The essay explores enjambment, indentation, and color symbolism as tools of political testimony. I am extremely proud of this work as a final essay for English 87.
